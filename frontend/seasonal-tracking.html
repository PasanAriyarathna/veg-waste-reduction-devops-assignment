🌾 Sri Lanka Cultivation & Harvest Seasons

Practical guide to vegetable seasons, growing areas & post-harvest insights

Maha Season (September - March)

Linked with North-East monsoon – The primary cultivation season across Sri Lanka. Peak planting and harvesting occur during this period, especially in up-country vegetable regions and irrigation-supported areas.

Key Insight: Up-country vegetables flourish during this season due to favorable monsoon rains and cooler temperatures. Low-country areas also see increased production with irrigation support.

Common Post-Harvest Loss Causes

🚚 Transport Damage

Poor packaging and over-stacking during transportation causes significant losses across the supply chain.

🌡️ Temperature Exposure

Lack of cold chain infrastructure leads to spoilage, especially in warm climates of low-country zones.

🏪 Handling at Wholesale/Retail

Poor storage practices and handling at markets and retail points contribute to quality degradation.

📦 Storage Conditions

Inadequate storage facilities with improper temperature and humidity control accelerate deterioration.

Annual Cultivation Timeline

January - March

End of Maha season. Harvesting peak for main crops. Transition planting begins for Yala season in some areas.

April - May

Short inter-season period. Yala season planting begins. Some areas continue extended Maha harvesting.

May - August

Yala season (dry season cultivation). Strong irrigation-dependent production. Low-country vegetables peak.

September - December

Maha season begins with North-East monsoon. Peak planting period. Up-country vegetables flourish.
